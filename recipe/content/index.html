FIG LEAF ICE CREAM

Ficus carica · frozen custard study

A custard-style ice cream infused with toasted fig leaves, which give the cream a delicate fig and coconut-like aroma. Fresh figs and pistachios keep the final dish simple and seasonal.

The leaves flavor the dairy through infusion; the fresh fruit is added at serving. This keeps the leaf aroma and the texture of the figs distinct.

Source
Beyond Sweet & Savory
Yield
8 servings
Timing
15 min prep + 20 min cook, plus steeping / chilling / freezing

Recipe at a Glance

  • Yield: 8 servings
  • Prep: 15 minutes
  • Cook: approximately 20 minutes
  • Infusion: at least 2 hours; up to 8 hours refrigerated
  • Cooling: approximately 30 minutes in an ice bath
  • Chilling: at least 4 hours, or overnight
  • Churning: approximately 25–30 minutes, depending on the machine
  • Freezing: at least 4 hours, until firm

Plan ahead: this recipe includes several separate waiting stages. Prepare the base the day before serving. Follow your machine’s instructions for freezing its bowl in advance.

INGREDIENTS

what goes in

Ice cream base

  • 2 oz fresh fig leaves, washed and dried
  • 2½ cups heavy cream
  • 1 cup full-fat milk
  • ½ cup granulated sugar, divided
  • 4 large egg yolks

For serving

  • 1 pint fresh figs, quartered
  • ¼ cup chopped pistachios

METHOD

toast → steep → churn
  1. Prepare the leaves: wash and dry them thoroughly. Heat the oven to 325°F. Toast the fig leaves directly on the center rack for about 8 minutes, until dry and fragrant but not burned.
  2. Warm the milk, cream, and ¼ cup sugar over low heat. Crumble in the toasted leaves and keep warm for 5 minutes without letting the mixture boil.
  3. Remove from heat, cover, and steep for at least 2 hours. For a stronger infusion, chill and steep overnight, up to 8 hours.
  4. Strain the infused dairy through a fine-mesh sieve into a clean saucepan, remove the leaves, and gently bring the dairy back to a simmer.
  5. Whisk the egg yolks with the remaining sugar until pale. Slowly whisk in hot cream in ¼-cup additions to temper the yolks, then return everything to the saucepan.
  6. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly, until the custard thickens enough to coat a spatula, about 15 minutes. Avoid boiling the custard.
  7. Strain the cooked custard through a fine-mesh sieve into a clean heatproof container.
  8. Cool the base in an ice bath for approximately 30 minutes, then refrigerate for at least 4 hours or overnight.
  9. Churn in an ice cream machine until soft-serve texture, about 25–30 minutes.
  10. Transfer to a freezer container, press parchment against the surface, seal, and freeze until firm, at least 4 hours.
  11. Serve with quartered fresh figs and chopped pistachios.

Recipe Notes

Ingredient notes

Use young, undamaged fig leaves. The leaves are strained out after infusion. Fresh figs are a serving ingredient, not a direct substitute for the leaves.

The sugar is divided: ¼ cup goes into the dairy and ¼ cup into the yolks. Keep the ice cream base and serving ingredients separate until serving.

Equipment and substitutions

Have a saucepan, heatproof bowl, whisk, spatula, fine-mesh sieve, ice bath, ice cream machine, and lidded freezer container ready. This is a churned custard recipe; a no-churn variation has not been tested for this project.

Pistachios are optional for serving; toasted coconut is another topping suggested by the source. The base contains dairy and eggs, and the original topping contains pistachios.

Storage

Keep in an airtight freezer container, with parchment against the surface. The source recommends eating it within two weeks for best quality and lists up to one month of freezer storage. Add fresh figs and toppings when serving.
