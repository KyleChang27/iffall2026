FIG LEAF ICE CREAM

Ficus carica · frozen custard study

A custard-style ice cream infused with toasted fig leaves, which give the cream a delicate fig and coconut-like aroma. Fresh figs and pistachios keep the final dish simple and seasonal.

Source
Beyond Sweet & Savory
Yield
8 servings
Timing
15 min prep + 20 min cook, plus steeping / chilling / freezing

INGREDIENTS

what goes in

Ice cream base

  • 2 oz fresh fig leaves, washed and dried
  • 2½ cups heavy cream
  • 1 cup full-fat milk
  • ½ cup granulated sugar, divided
  • 4 large egg yolks

For serving

  • 1 pint fresh figs, quartered
  • ¼ cup chopped pistachios

METHOD

toast → steep → churn
  1. Heat the oven to 325°F. Toast the fig leaves directly on the center rack for about 8 minutes, until dry and fragrant but not burned.
  2. Warm the milk, cream, and ¼ cup sugar over low heat. Crumble in the toasted leaves and keep warm for 5 minutes without letting the mixture boil.
  3. Remove from heat, cover, and steep for at least 2 hours. For a stronger infusion, chill and steep overnight, up to 8 hours.
  4. Strain the infused dairy into a clean saucepan and gently bring it back to a simmer.
  5. Whisk the egg yolks with the remaining sugar until pale. Slowly whisk in small amounts of hot cream to temper the yolks, then return everything to the saucepan.
  6. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly, until the custard thickens enough to coat a spatula, about 15 minutes. Strain again.
  7. Cool the base in an ice bath, then refrigerate for at least 4 hours or overnight.
  8. Churn in an ice cream machine until soft-serve texture, about 25–30 minutes. Transfer to a container and freeze until firm, at least 4 hours.
  9. Serve with quartered fresh figs and chopped pistachios.

RECIPE SITES

hierarchy + usability

cooking.nytimes.com

NYT Cooking

NYT Cooking separates photography, recipe metadata, ingredients, and method very clearly, so the page stays easy to scan even when the recipe is detailed. I would like to borrow that clarity without making the final page feel like a standard recipe database.

food52.com

Food52

Food52 lets photography carry more of the personality while the recipe information remains straightforward. That balance is useful for this project because the fig leaves are visually distinctive enough to become part of the identity of the page.

seriouseats.com

Serious Eats

Serious Eats is a useful reference for communicating technique and long processes. The recipe has several waiting stages—steeping, chilling, churning, freezing—so I want the instructions to remain practical even if the visual direction is more experimental.

OTHER SITES

visual systems + research

are.na

Are.na

I like the way Are.na presents collected images and ideas as individual blocks rather than forcing them into a conventional article layout. That system suggests a research-board approach where process photos, ingredient notes, and recipe information can feel collected and connected.

kew.org

Kew Herbarium

The herbarium treats plants as specimens that are labelled, catalogued, and studied. I want to borrow that informational tone—small labels, botanical naming, and precise image framing—so the fig leaf becomes the central subject rather than just a garnish.

mackbooks.co.uk

MACK

MACK’s site gives images a lot of room while keeping typography and navigation very controlled. Its book-like pacing is a useful reference for making the final recipe feel designed and image-led without relying on the same large-serif editorial treatment as many food sites.
